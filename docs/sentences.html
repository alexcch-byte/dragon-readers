Sentence Lab

Ready for more? Spot the naming, action and describing words, sort the parts of a sentence, decide what is a whole sentence, then build your own. For readers about 6 to 8.

    ⭐
    For grown-ups

    Best for children who can already read simple words and short sentences. Sit beside them for the first few sentences, then let them try alone. There are no scores that go down, no timers and no streaks. A wrong choice just gets a kind nudge and the sentence stays open. “Show me” is always there, and looking is not a failure.

    • Level 1, naming words (nouns). A noun names a person, animal, place or thing. Ask: “Can you point to it, or picture it?” “Which words are the names of things?”
    • Level 2, the action word (verb). A verb tells what is happening. Ask: “Which word tells us what is happening?” “Can you act it out?”
    • Level 3, describing words (adjectives). An adjective tells more about a person or thing: what it is like (big, soft, tired, hungry, cozy). Ask: “Which word tells us more about the thing?” “What changes if we take it out?”
    • Level 4, who and what. The subject is who or what the sentence is about, the verb is the action, and the object is what they did it to. Not every sentence has an object (“The baby slept.”), but these ones do. The boxes change places each time, so reading matters. Ask: “Who or what is this about?” “What did they do?” “What did they do it to?”
    • Level 5, is it a sentence? A sentence is a complete thought: it tells who or what, and what happens. A group of words that is missing one of those is only a piece of a sentence (“The big dog.” or “In the park.”), even with a capital letter and a period. Ask: “Who or what is it about?” “What happens?” “Does it feel finished?”
    • Level 6, build a sentence. A sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a period (telling), a question mark (asking) or an exclamation mark (a big feeling). Ask: “Does it sound right when you read it aloud?” “Are we telling or asking?”

    Sentences are read aloud with your browser’s voice, and a voice stored on the device is preferred. Some browsers only offer online voices, and those may send the sentence text to a speech service. If there is no voice, everything still works silently. Only which levels are finished may be remembered on this device. There are no accounts, no ads and no tracking.
